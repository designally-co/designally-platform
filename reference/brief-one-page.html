For the creative team

  1. The real tension is state-enterprise credibility versus startup energy. Stakeholders want the trust of PTT and the boldness of Tesla at once. The likely answer is a professional, modern base with controlled boldness — not playfulness.
  2. Tesla was named by twelve people, but never for how it looks. Every reason given was about speed, being a trend-setter, and communicating boldly. The reference means behave like Tesla, not look like Tesla — do not bring a minimal-white moodboard on that basis.
  3. The famous-person answers split into two camps that match the tone conflict exactly: Steve Jobs, Elon Musk, Nikola Tesla, Christopher Columbus (innovator) against Taylor Swift, Lisa, Justin Bieber (performer). The same people who chose the performers chose the "Fun" adjectives.
  4. On the ten personality scales, eight are near-unanimous and only two are split — Serious–Fun and Subtle–Bold. That makes the kick-off discussion a five-minute item, not an hour.

How to run the room

🔒 INTERNAL — never shown to the client

Open with the B2B/B2C question and do not move on until it is answered. Everything else in the project depends on it, and the room will try to avoid it because it is uncomfortable.

Ask who decides, in the first five minutes. Nobody claimed final authority in the survey. If that person is not in the meeting, book a second session rather than designing on an unresolved split.

Handle the tone conflict gently. Both camps put the other's preference on their avoid list, so framing it as a spectrum with dots on a line will work better than asking people to argue for a side.
